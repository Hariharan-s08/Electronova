Six tracks, one deliverable: a working build

Pick one when you register. Problem statements go to shortlisted teams a week before the event, so you can plan your circuit before you arrive.

IoT & IIoT

Connected sensor nodes, industrial monitoring, smart campus and factory-floor devices — anything that reads the world and reports back over a network.

Deliver: powered prototype streaming live sensor data

5G/6G & Wireless Communication

LoRa links, mesh networks, antenna experiments, spectrum sensing, software-defined radio, next-gen wireless concepts.

Deliver: two nodes talking across the lab

Embedded Systems & Robotics

Microcontroller-driven machines — motion control, sensing and actuation, autonomous behaviour on real hardware.

Deliver: a system that moves or reacts, on the bench

Digital Systems & VLSI

RTL on FPGA, accelerator blocks, verification flows, open-source EDA, digital logic that has to actually clock in.

Vital-sign sensing, wearable form factors, assistive devices, signal chains that run on real captured data, not a saved plot.

Deliver: a working wearable or bedside prototype

Questions

I have never soldered. Can I still take part?

Yes. There is a short soldering demo at check-in, and lab technicians are at the station all 24 hours to guide you through your first joints.

Do we have to build hardware, or can we submit only software?

Every track ends in something the jury can watch run. For software-heavy tracks that can be your code running on the supplied board, but a laptop-only submission is not accepted.

Is accommodation provided for outstation teams?

Yes, separate hostel accommodation is arranged for the night of 22 October for teams travelling more than 100 km. Mention it when you reply to the confirmation email.

Can we bring a project we already started?

Bring your research, datasets, and hardware, but assemble and write it during the event. Declare anything pre-built at check-in and the jury will judge only what you add.

What if the build does not finish?

Demo what runs. The jury is told to reward a partly working system the team understands over a finished one it cannot explain.
